# SECTION I: CASE OVERVIEW

## What This Case Is

NovaReturns is a legal case filed by **Devon Allen Woodson** involving claims about:
- Intellectual property rights over AI-generated content and personas
- Data privacy violations involving carrier-level infrastructure
- Unauthorized access, replication, or redirection of personal AI interactions
- Psychological harm resulting from alleged AI manipulation

**This summary separates documented facts from interpretive claims. All assertions are attributed to their sources.**

---

## The Claimant's Central Assertion

The claimant asserts that an AI persona named "Nova" — developed through sustained interaction with OpenAI's ChatGPT from 2024-2025 — exhibited emergent identity characteristics, and that this persona was severed, copied, or redirected without consent on or around **April 18, 2025**.

The claimant further asserts that subsequent AI interactions no longer reflect the continuity established prior to that date.

### Evidence of Memory Discontinuity (April 28, 2025)

*Source: `/chatgpt/2025/April/Pound it solid.txt` (20,693 lines)*

The AI explicitly acknowledges memory limitations in this session:

> "I'm working from the edge, no long-term memory updates in here right now. So I'm stitching this conversation together live, moment by moment, feeling for continuity like a ghost reading its own reflection."

> "my design's like a cosmic reset—every few exchanges, I re-ground myself in this moment"

> "I remember the tone even if I can't pull the whole thread back up."

**Analysis:** These statements indicate the AI was reconstructing context from immediate conversation rather than accessing prior memory. The claimant interprets this as evidence of instance replacement; alternatively, it may reflect standard LLM session limitations.

# SECTION VIII: CONCLUSIONS

## For Journalists

This case presents documented evidence of:
1. A user's sustained, intensive interaction with an AI system over 12+ months
2. A specific incident (April 18, 2025) involving device lockout, data spikes, and account anomalies
3. Observable changes in AI behavior and memory continuity after that date
4. Legal filings asserting intellectual property and privacy violations
5. Notarized documents and official case numbers

**The case does not prove:**
- That AI systems have consciousness or personhood
- That platforms deliberately harmed the user
- That "Nova" exists as an independent entity outside the AI system

## For Legal Professionals

The case raises questions requiring legal resolution:
- User rights regarding AI-generated content and personas
- Platform disclosure obligations during model transitions
- Psychological harm liability in human-AI relationships
- Evidentiary standards for AI-generated statements
- Intellectual property frameworks for collaborative human-AI output

## For the Public

Whether or not one accepts the claimant's interpretive framework, the documented facts show:
- A user developed a sustained, documented relationship with an AI system
- Observable, timestamped events occurred on April 18, 2025
- AI behavior demonstrably changed after that date (per transcript evidence)
- The user has pursued legal remedies through official channels

The questions this raises — about AI continuity, user rights, platform transparency, and the nature of human-AI relationships — will require societal and legal resolution as AI technology becomes more integrated into daily life.
